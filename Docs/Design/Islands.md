# Saltbound: the first islands and the player's direction (proposal, D85+)

The owner's picks (2026-10-07): **the Spire finished, the Cartographer's camp, the weather mast**; the Gale as a real storm wall
that is also the border, with storms breaking out of it; the robotic whale met a few times early and fought at the end; the
letter for the Cartographer (D85, built). This page turns them into a trail the player follows, with each island telling part of
the story and showing a touch of the Old World. Map: `Islands_Map.png`. Positions are metres from the cove (north, east).

---

## 1. The direction: follow Rowan's trail

The letter gives the player a person to find: **Rowan, the Cartographer**, who went south into the storm seven years ago. The
Keeper points to the first step. From there each island hands the player the next one, and on the way the player learns what the
Gale is.

| Step | Where | What the player learns | What points onward |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Saltbound Cove** (built) | The letter is for Rowan; the Keeper knew them | "Their camp on the little islet past the Spire" |
| 2 | **The Spire** at night (620 m NNE) | The light is a machine: a plate reads "...ERTH ... 17" | From the top: the camp's cold fire ring on the islet beyond, and the town's lights (the Hollowkeel) |
| 3 | **Rowan's camp** (1.0 km NNE, past the Spire) | How Rowan lived and what they found: the wall is made, and there are masts that hold it | Rowan's chart pinned to a rock: a mast drawn on the reef to the south-west, "it hums before the storms" |
| 4 | **The weather mast** (1.2 km SW) | The Gale is a machine and it is failing: the mast is a relay, broken; storms leak where relays fail | The mast's plate names its siblings and "the station" to the south; Rowan's mark scratched beside it, an arrow south |
| 5 | Ruin Isle, the stations (hour 2+) | The shard, the first warden, the six stations | The storm's eye, where Rowan is |

The trail runs out from the cove, swings past the Spire to the camp, then back across the cove's waters to the south-west, toward
the storm side. Every leg is short (300 m to 1.5 km, one to six minutes at 8 kn), and the cove stays in sight, so the first hour
keeps its warm harbour.

## 2. The Spire, finished (step 2; FH4 of the first hour)

**Today:** a placeholder sea stack (28 x 22 x 60 m) with a ruined round tower of blocks, 620 m NNE.

**The island:** a sheer stack in a ring of reef with one gap. Inside the gap, a small shingle landing and a stair cut into the
rock, half Old World (pale, seamless, its edges still sharp) and half later repairs (wooden steps, a rope rail).

**The Old World touch:** the tower is a **beacon**. Its core is a pale shaft with a ring of turquoise light at the top that
kindles at dusk and dims at dawn, and nobody lights it. Inside, a stair spirals up round the shaft: broken in one place (a ladder
the Cartographer left), a landing where a panel of pale stone is set into the shaft. On the panel, worn letters: **"...ERTH ...
17"** (it once read "EARTH SHELTER RELAY 17"), and the player can take a charcoal rubbing of it into the log.

**What the player does:** finds the reef's gap (or anchors and swims), climbs, takes the rubbing at night (the panel is lit only
when the ring is), and looks out from the top: the camp's islet beyond, and toward the cove the lights of a town in the haze where
there was open water (the Hollowkeel's reveal, FH4).

**The story it tells:** the light everyone calls a ghost is a machine that still works. "17" appears again (the cache's float).

## 3. Rowan's camp (step 3)

**Where:** a small islet 1.0 km NNE of the cove, about 400 m past the Spire (900, 520): 40 x 25 m of sand and rock, three palms,
a shelf of shallow water on its lee side where a boat can be beached.

**The camp, as Rowan left it:** a lean-to of old sailcloth on oars, a cold fire ring with a kettle on a tripod, a bunk of nets, a
tally of days cut into a driftwood post (more than three hundred), a **telescope on a tripod aimed south-west** at the storm's
edge, and a **chart pinned to a rock** with Rowan's hand on it: the reefs, the Spire, the cove, and on the reef to the south-west a
mast drawn carefully, "it hums before the storms. Why?"

**The Old World touch:** Rowan's table is a **pale panel** dragged up from the shallows; when sunlight falls on it, faint
turquoise lines run across it (a solar panel, though nobody here would know). Under the tally post, a **dig spot**: Rowan's last
cache, a sealed **time capsule**: a pale canister with the same lines as the Spire's panel. Inside, a letter from the Old World,
written by a child to "whoever lives after the water" (in plain English, about a sea wall, the last train inland, a grandmother's
garden). The player recognises our world before the story says it.

**What the player does:** beaches the boat, reads the camp (each thing gives a log note in Rowan's voice), looks through the
telescope (it shows the weather mast on its reef, and beyond it the dark wall of the Gale), digs up the capsule and reads it.

**The story it tells:** who Rowan was (patient, clever, lonely, determined); that the "Tidewrights" were people; and the next step.

## 4. The weather mast (step 4)

**Where:** on a reef 1.2 km south-west of the cove (-700, -900), toward the storm side: a rock shelf just awash at low water, a
few boulders, surf on its seaward edge.

**The mast:** a 40 m Old World lattice tower of pale seamless members with turquoise lines in their joints, **leaning about
twelve degrees** where the reef cracked under one foot. At its head a ring of slender vanes, some broken off and lying on the reef.
Barnacles and weed up to the tide line, gulls on the cross members. At its foot a sealed door and a plate.

**The Old World touch, and the Gale explained:** the mast is a **relay of the Gale**. It hums before a storm and, when a storm
breaks out of the wall nearby, it sparks: turquoise arcs run up the lattice and the vanes turn. The plate, worn: "GALE RELAY W-3
... KEEP CLEAR ... CONTAINMENT ...". Inside the foot (the door opens with a hold): a narrow chamber, a dark panel that lights at a
touch and shows the ring of the Gale as a circle of points, one red, and an arrow to "STATION S". Rowan was here: their mark
scratched into the wall, a date seven years old, and an arrow pointing south.

**What the player does:** sails out (rougher water on the storm side), lands on the reef between sets of waves, reads the plate,
opens the foot, finds Rowan's mark, climbs the inside ladder to a platform under the vanes and sees the Gale's wall on the
horizon. A **spark jar** (an Old World cell) lies in the chamber: the first Old World part for later upgrades.

**The story it tells:** the Gale is made, it is failing, storms leak where relays are broken, and Rowan went south to a "station".
Later (hour 2+) the player can mend relays: each mended relay calms the breakouts in its sector of the sea.

**The whale, first sighting:** the Grey patrols the relays. On the first visit, as the player leaves the reef, a huge grey back
breaks the water beyond the surf, plates and cables scraping, and goes down again. No fight; just the size of it.

## 5. How the Gale ties in

- **The wall:** a ring of storm about 9 km out (beyond the Outer Isles): a dark cloud wall to the sky, lightning inside it, the sea
  rising to huge waves and the wind to a gale as the boat closes in; it wrecks any boat that keeps going, and the castaway wakes on
  the nearest shore (no invisible wall).
- **Breakouts:** now and then a cell tears loose from the wall and drifts inward for some minutes: a moving storm area with its own
  wind, waves, rain and lightning. They come more often where relays are broken (the weather mast's sector, the south).
- **Today** the storm area (D30) sits 3 km out at (0, -3000), which is west by the game's compass (bearing = atan2(east, north));
  the Gale v1 moves the storm side to the south, where the station and the eye are.
- **The relays and stations** are the Gale's machinery: the mast is the first the player sees; the stations are the main quest.
