Rules

Five rules for every screen

The sea never stops

Only Esc pauses the game. You open the satchel, chart and logbook while the boat sails on, so you look up from the chart now and then. That keeps you sailing.

If you carry it, you hold it

The compass, spyglass, chart and lantern are in your hand. They are not icons in a corner of the screen. You keep one tool out at a time.

An empty screen while sailing

No health bars, minimap or speed gauge. You read the wind from the pennant and the sail, your heading from the compass, and the time of day from the sun.

Paper, leather, brass, ink

Each piece looks like something from the boat: a stitched bag, a hand-drawn chart, a sea-stained book, a crate. There are no floating panels and no frames around them.

You draw the chart

The chart starts blank. Ink appears along your wake. Islands seen on the horizon show up dotted with a question mark, and you name them by landing on them.

Your call

Decisions before anything gets built

1. Are the objects drawn flat or held in 3D hands?

  1. Painted 2D objects on screen, as in this mockup. Quick to change, and they still look like things.Start here
  2. First-person arms holding real 3D models (the Sea of Thieves way). Looks best, but needs arms, animations and a model for every tool.

2. Does the chart fill in as you sail?

  1. Yes. It starts blank and ink follows your wake. Sighted islands appear dotted.Recommended
  2. No. Fully drawn from the start, like today.

3. How do you pick tools?

  1. Tool wheel on Tab, plus number keys 1–5.Recommended
  2. Number keys only, no wheel.

4. Does the satchel grow?

  1. 24 pockets now; a bigger bag later, made from rope and leather you find.Recommended
  2. Always 24.

5. Does the logbook write itself?

  1. Yes: short entries for landfall, first finds, storms and mooring.Recommended
  2. Only lists (places and items), no written entries.
