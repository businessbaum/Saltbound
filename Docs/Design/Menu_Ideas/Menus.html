Ideas
  • The world stops and blurs; nothing else covers it. The game only pauses here, never in the satchel or the wheel.
  • A "where you are" card answers the questions you have after a break: where am I, where is my boat, when did I last save.
  • "Save now" is only offered where saving makes sense (moored or anchored); elsewhere it is greyed with the reason.
  • Quitting asks for confirmation only if something happened since the last save.

Controls
  • Sliders are a length of rope with the knot as the handle; the number sits at the end.
  • Choices are "‹ value ›", so every setting works with A / D and a gamepad.
  • On / Off: the active word is inked and underlined.
Saltbound touches
  • Quality presets named after the sea: Calm, Fair, Fresh, Gale.
  • Distances in metres or in cables and nautical miles; headings in degrees or in compass points ("NE by E").
  • "Music at sea: often, sometimes, never" for players who sail by the sound of the water.
Accessibility
  • Seasickness comfort: a steady horizon, less camera roll with the boat, a soft vignette in sharp turns.
  • Every hold action can become press-to-toggle.
  • Bigger text, subtitles on a parchment backing, high-contrast prompts.

Ideas
  • Each voyage is a page with a print of where you last were, the day and the place: easy to tell apart.
  • Deleting uses the hold you already know: the ink soaks the X key while you hold it. No extra "are you sure?" box.
  • Three voyages are plenty for a game like this; the boat and the world are saved per voyage.

Ideas
  • One small scrap of parchment in the middle; the world stays visible around it.

  • It says what you would lose, in time, not in a warning.

The first-start steps
  • 1 · Brightness with the logo as the test pattern: three knots from clear to invisible.
  • 2 · Text size and subtitles, shown on a real prompt and a real line.
  • 3 · Seasickness comfort: a short sway preview and one question.
  • Then the intro plays and the voyage starts at dawn in the cove. Every step can be skipped and found again in Settings.

Ideas
  • Keys are the same parchment scraps as the prompts, so what you set here is exactly what you see in the game.
  • While waiting for a key, a quill caret blinks; a clash is explained on a small scrap with a one-key answer.
  • Gamepad layouts show the right face-button names for the pad that is plugged in.

More ideas

Small things that make it feel finished

The menu is your last saveThe world behind the start menu is where you left off, at its real time of day. A new voyage starts at dawn in the cove.
Prompts that step backA setting "Prompts: full · fade once learned · off". After you have used a station a few times, its prompt shrinks to the key alone.
The helm slippedWhen a gamepad disconnects: the game pauses on a parchment scrap, "The helm slipped from your hands. Press any button to take it again."
Subtitles on scrapsSubtitles in painted lettering, or on a parchment backing for readability; speaker names in small caps.
Quit without a fussIf nothing happened since the last save, Quit just quits, with the save sign for a moment. The question only comes when there is something to lose.
Photo mode, laterHide the hands and the HUD, tilt the horizon, add a vignette; the picture becomes a print you can paste into the logbook.
Sounds for every screenRope creak on moving, a soft bell on choosing, paper on scraps, the sea always under it. The menus never go silent.
Same keys everywhereE chooses, Esc goes back, Q / E change pages, A / D change values, in every screen; gamepad A / B / bumpers the same.
Ideas only: nothing here is part of the build spec. The game frames use a real capture from Saltbound; the logo, prompts and rope are the chosen designs.
