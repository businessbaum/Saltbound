The first hour's new screens

What M6 still needs on the screen, drawn over the game with the approved pages' own drawing kit (the satchel's stitched leather and brass, the prompt's torn parchment and key scrap, the crate's wood and iron, wax seals and ink): the people's lines, the Shipwright's plans (upgrades from materials and salt), the log, and the job board. Every part is a thing from the boat or the harbour. The prompts of digging and repairs (D73, D74) already use the existing key scrap and its ink-soak hold, so they are not drawn again. Recommendations are marked; the questions are at the end.

Drawn at 1920 × 1080 like the approved designs (python Tools/design/make_first_hour.py), so the parts can be cut out for the game the same way as the satchel's (Tools/textures/gen_ui_parts.py).

1 · A person speaks

A · low in the middle, like the prompt
Idea A Recommended
  • One torn half sheet low in the middle, where the prompt sits. The speaker's mark in red wax at its corner (the Harbour Keeper's anchor; each person has their own), the name in small capitals over a quill rule, the line in ink.
  • No portrait: the person stands in the world in front of you (round 2: figures mostly behind counters, text on parchment, murmurs instead of voices).
  • Choices are keys on small torn scraps, as the prompt's E: 1, 2, 3 for questions, and E always the plain answer that ends it. The chosen one is underlined in ink and lit like warm paper. W / S and the mouse choose too.
  • The world keeps running (only Esc pauses, as with the satchel). Walking away ends it.
  • A murmur per line (a few syllables of the person's voice), not words.
B · pinned beside the speaker
Idea B
  • A small scrap beside the speaker, held by a brass tack and a twine to them, like the prompt on its object (D60): the line belongs to a place in the world.
  • Good for short calls and passers-by ("Mind the reef"); long talks and choices are cramped.
  • Could be the form for lines people say while you pass, with A for talks you start.

2 · The Shipwright's plans

E at the Shipwright's board at the Slip
The plan board Recommended
  • The Shipwright's board at the Slip (D64): a framed board of planks with iron corners and a burnt name board. The plans are slips tacked down the left; the chosen one lifts off the wood in warm light.
  • The chosen plan pinned large in his hand: what it is, an ink drawing of the work (here her planks in section, the seams payed with tar, the pot and brush, a scale bar) and what it does for her, in plain words.
  • What it takes lies on a stitched leather roll across the sheet's foot, its pockets pressed rings like the satchel's (D55): the item, and what you have / what it needs on a brass-rimmed pill, red when short.
  • Salt is a bar of rock salt bound with a fibre band (the currency, D61), its count on the satchel's coin; the purse sits bottom left. The materials come from the satchel and the boat's stores.
  • "Have it fitted": he takes what it needs; she must lie at the fitting berth, and the next time you come aboard it is on her (the concept's 6.3).
  • Done plans are struck through and sealed in red wax; locked ones are greyed with a lock and say why on hover ("He wants to see her in a rough sea first").
  • M6's three: reef points, tarred seams, a new sail (the sail-age tier). The Tinker's Old World plans use the same board later, on darker paper with a turquoise rule. His dividers and pencil lie on the board.

3 · The log

L · the log
The log Recommended
  • A dark leather-bound book with stitched edges, brass corners, a ribbon marker and its strap and buckle lying open.
  • Left: the Cartographer's pages in their own slanted hand, with their sketches (the leaning palm, three paces seaward, the red cross), a water ring, a torn corner.
  • Right: your notes write themselves: places, people, finds, clues, jobs (the concept's 3.6), each with a small inked mark and the time of day from the clock (D72). Things you take, like the rubbing from the Spire's stone, are pasted in.
  • The rubbing is the first quiet hint of the Old World: block letters no one on the islands writes, and the 17 again (the pale float's number).
  • Leather tabs: Pages (the Cartographer's, about 20), Notes, Words (the Old World's writing, rubbings and your guesses, 6.5), Places, Plans (the ones you own).
  • Its own key (L) and a way in from the satchel; the world keeps running. The loading screen's "From an old log" lines (D66) come from the pages found.

4 · The job board

E at the board in the Tally House
The board Recommended
  • The Tally House's board (D61) under its burnt name board: handwritten slips on iron nails, folded once, with what is left of slips torn down and empty nail holes between them. The one looked at lifts off the wood in warm light.
  • Each slip says what, where in sailor's words, a small ink sketch, the kind (bring, deliver, survey, sighting, dig, salvage, search) and the pay in salt.
  • Taken ones get the Purser's red wax seal and go into your log's notes; you hand in at the pay hatch (the chalk slate says so).
  • M6's three kinds: bring, survey, dig (the concept's 10.2). No job repeats a trip.
